Civils Avoidance Guide

A field-led guide to support technicians with avoiding unnecessary civils by completing the right checks, using the correct tools, capturing clear evidence and escalating with a strong rationale only when required.

What good looks like

Strong civils rationale

  • All reasonable route recovery options have been attempted before escalation.
  • The technician can clearly explain where the blockage or restriction has been identified.
  • Evidence supports the need for civils, including photos, notes and test results where applicable.
  • The correct tools have been attempted before concluding civils is required.
  • The escalation clearly explains why the route is not recoverable using standard field activity.
What to avoid

Weak civils rationale

  • Assuming civils is required without attempting recovery tools first.
  • Providing limited notes such as “blocked duct” without clear evidence.
  • Not narrowing down the location of the blockage.
  • Escalating without confirming what has already been attempted.
  • Raising civils as the first option rather than the last resort.
Before booking civils

Pre-booking checks

  • Confirm the correct build type and understand the expected route before progressing.
  • Check available network records, maps or previous notes where applicable.
  • Attempt all safe and approved route recovery options before escalation.
  • Capture clear evidence showing what has been attempted and where the issue appears to be.
  • Escalate with a clear explanation before civils is raised.
Why it matters

Why civils avoidance matters

  • Reduces unnecessary cost and avoids avoidable follow-on work.
  • Improves customer experience by helping achieve resolution sooner.
  • Supports completion by using the right tools and checks before escalation.
  • Improves consistency across teams and regions.
  • Ensures civils is only raised when it is genuinely required.

Generic Hints + Tips

Use this section for common reminders, quick checks and good practice before civils is raised.

  • Do not rely on short notes alone — explain what has been checked.
  • Photos should clearly show the issue, location and attempted recovery where possible.
  • Consider whether a temporary solution can support completion while the permanent fix progresses.
  • When unsure, escalate with evidence before booking civils.
